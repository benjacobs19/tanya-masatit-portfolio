Case study · @sheistanyam · Feb 2026

3 things I learned from 3M+ organic views

Over two months I tested one content idea on my own Instagram. Same concept, split into two parts, zero paid promotion. The results changed how I think about marketing, and this is the method behind them.

01

One concept, two parts

Every idea ships as Part 1 and Part 2. The sequencing gives people a reason to follow, and the second post inherits the first one's momentum.

02

Test the hook, not the topic

I cut two or three opening lines for the same video and watch the first-three-second retention before I decide which one stays.

03

Score by shares and saves

Likes tell me it was liked. Shares tell me it travelled, saves tell me it was useful. Those two numbers decide what I make next.

04

Re-cut the winner

The best-performing structure becomes a template: same pacing, same on-screen text rhythm, new subject. That's how a one-off becomes a series.

Lesson one

Shares beat likes.

Likes boost ego. Shares build distribution. When someone sends your post to a friend, the algorithm reads it as value.

"Virality isn't about being liked. It's about being passed along."

Lesson two

Saves signal long-term value.

A save tells the platform this is worth coming back to. Content that teaches, simplifies or organizes gets saved.

"Entertainment gets attention. Clarity gets stored."

Lesson three

Structure beats creativity.

The hook, the pacing, the Part 1 → Part 2 sequencing. Most brands focus on visuals; few focus on psychology.

"Retention is engineered."
